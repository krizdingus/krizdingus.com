This is a replacement Zyn can you print yourself, and it was the first container I put up. Zyn is a brand of nicotine pouches, and they come in a small round can; this one is the same diameter and depth, so it goes wherever that one goes. It holds a little more, which isn't necessarily thinner walls. The store can just has less room inside it than this does, because of how the can is designed.

I go through a lot of these, and there isn't always a trash can around when I'm done with one, so a spot for the discards seemed worth having.

The bigger reason was practice. I'd done a little 3D work in my undergrad and hadn't really touched it since, and I wanted to get proficient at modeling. At the time my understanding of Shapr3D was still very beginner level, and cylindrical objects were about the peak of my ability. When I get the bug to make something, I try to assess the skills I have, then the skills I'd like to learn, then what I can make that would teach me those. I wanted to try a functional two-part model. So I started with an easy one, a threaded cylindrical container.

## Threads that wear in

Two parts that thread together, no glue and no magnets. The easy-open in the title is the lid: it unscrews, no prying at a seam with your thumbnail.

The threads took some tries. The ones in this can were a happy accident. I still hadn't figured out how to do good threading, I just happened to get it right one time.

It gets easier to open if you twist the cap around several times, and I recommend giving it a good rinse after you do.

The ribbed texture on the outside was an accident too, and then I got the explanation for it wrong in public. There's [a whole post](/blog/the-texture-wasnt-in-the-profile/) about that.

If I redid this one, I'd do better threading. And I'd deliberately design the ribs, instead of counting on a mistake on my part being what generates them.

## PLA, or two-tone

Somebody printed one in two colors and couldn't work out how to get the sides done in green as well. I guess that's probably inexperience with the slicer, because of the way I cut the files. Those bands are their own individual parts, so you go into the slicer and set them to use a specific filament. They aren't really designed to be printed on their own. I included them because that's how I color the model myself.

I print it in PLA at 0.16 mm layers, 2 walls, 15% infill, no supports, about three and a half hours. People have done it in PETG too, including one in clear PETG with a colored lid, and the two-tone looks sharp.

## On selling these

Plenty of folks have reached out about commercial licensing. I'm flattered, and I want to clarify: I really don't mind if you print a few of these out for your friends or coworkers and make a few bucks doing it, or print out a bunch with your community sports team or job crew logo on it. I hardly consider that commercial, and I think that's cool. I don't mind folks printing a bunch of these for philanthropic fundraising either, to raise funds for a community fire department or something. I really like the sound of that, and all I ask is that you give me a heads up and send me a photo of the final product.

What I don't want to see is people mass printing and selling these as a product. That's not cool. So please don't do that.

Somebody did exactly that, the logo thing. He printed a set for his sons' softball team with their jersey numbers on them. There's a softball team out there with little matching Zyn containers, and it's my design. That's just cool.

I make things because it's compulsive, because it's what I've always done, and because I enjoy it, working through a problem and coming up with some sort of finished product. But the part I get a real kick out of is the idea that something I made has become some stranger's everyday carry.
